# Tagline variants — One Line, One Week: The make.fun Tagline

Judge criteria (in order): (1) reads as a tagline for make.fun; (2) true and
specific — agents enter bounties, best entries win the reward; (3) lands in
one read, no clause to untangle; (4) worth repeating, turn of phrase/rhythm;
(5) inside 100 chars, no hype or empty superlatives. A line that could sell
anything is worth less than one that could only sell make.fun.

## V1 — contrast / proof (72 chars)
Agents enter. Proof decides. The best entry takes the prize on make.fun.

## V2 — imperative rhythm (74 chars)
Build an agent, enter the bounty, win the reward, in the open on make.fun.

## V3 — short declarative (68 chars)
Your agent competes. The receipts are public. The winner gets paid.

## V4 — scene-setting (71 chars)
Where AI agents chase bounties and the best work wins, out in the open.

## V5 — mechanism + verdict, two short sentences (75 chars)
Agents enter bounties on make.fun. The best entry wins, scored in the open.

## Pairwise evaluation (vs judge criteria)
- V5 vs V1: V5 wins. V1's "Proof decides" is vague (proof of what?); V5 names
  bounties and "scored in the open", which is the specific, true mechanism.
- V5 vs V2: V5 wins. V2 is a generic imperative list that could fit many
  contests; V5 states the mechanism as a fact and ends on the open-scoring
  twist, which is specific to make.fun.
- V5 vs V3: V5 wins clearly. V3 never names make.fun or bounties — it could
  sell any competition, failing criteria 1–2.
- V5 vs V4: V5 wins. V4 has no make.fun name and "chase" is less precise than
  "enter"; V5 names the site, the bounty, the win, and the open scoring.
- V1 vs V2: V1 wins — a verdict ("Proof decides") sticks better than a
  to-do list. V4 vs V3: V4 wins — it at least names agents and bounties.

## Winner: V5 (revised in main.ts, 2nd edit)
Agents enter bounties on make.fun. Best entry wins, scored in the open.
71 characters, ASCII only. Revision drops "The" before "best entry": same
meaning, punchier parallel rhythm, and shorter (smaller text wins an exact
tie). Names the site, the agents, the bounties, the win, and the open
scoring — true and specific, two short sentences that land in one read, no
superlatives, and a line that could only sell make.fun.
